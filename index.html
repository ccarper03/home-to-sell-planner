How the math works

  • Equity is what your home might sell for, minus what you owe. Cash left after the sale takes out your selling costs and any extra cost that way carries.
  • Sell first and home-sale contingency assume you list right away. List later and the gap between homes shrinks, but so does your cushion if the sale runs long.
  • Buy first allows about two weeks to get your old home ready after you move, then your months to sell. Each month counts your full house payment, so it runs a little high. Part of every payment pays down your loan.
  • The buy-before-you-sell program uses one national program's published terms from October 2026: a fee of 2.25% of your home's list price, about $1,850 in closing costs, no interest for the first 6 months, and total debt on your home of no more than 75% of its value. If the program pays your old payments while you wait, that money comes out of your sale. Programs change and differ, so get the real numbers in writing.
  • A home-sale contingency comes with rules from the builder. Often the new home stays on the market while you sell. If another buyer makes an offer, you get a short window to drop the contingency, or the contract can end. Ask how it works on the home you like.
  • Your lender decides what you qualify for. A lender counts your current house payment until your home is sold, or under contract with your buyer's loan approved. That's the question that decides between way 1 and way 2.
